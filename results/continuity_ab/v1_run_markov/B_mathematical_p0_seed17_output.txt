Leaves have specialized structures called chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll primarily absorbs light in the blue and red parts of the visible spectrum and reflects green light. Therefore, when sunlight hits a leaf, the chlorophyll molecules in the leaves absorb the blue and red light, but the green light is scattered back into the air around the leaf, giving it its characteristic green color.

In addition to chlorophyll, leaves also contain other pigments such as carotenoids and anthocyanins, which contribute to the leaf's coloration. However, the green color is the most prominent and is what we typically perceive as the color of leaves.
