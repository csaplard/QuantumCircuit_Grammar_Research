Strong tides are called "spring tides." Spring tides occur twice a month, about every 12 hours and 25 minutes, and are the highest and lowest tides of the month. They are caused by the gravitational forces of the Moon and the Sun.

--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
Both the Sun and the Moon contribute to the tides on Earth. The Moon's gravitational pull is the primary cause of the daily tides we experience, but the Sun also has a significant effect, especially during full and new moons. The combined gravitational forces of the Moon and the Sun create the high tides we see during spring tides, which are higher than normal high tides and lower than normal low tides.
