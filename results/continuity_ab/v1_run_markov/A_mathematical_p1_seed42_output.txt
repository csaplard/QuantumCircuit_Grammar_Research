There are several reasons why plants are primarily adapted to use carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O) for photosynthesis:

1. Light absorption: Plants absorb light energy from the sun through their leaves, stems, and other parts. CO2 and H2O are important for capturing and using this light energy to perform photosynthesis.

2. Carbon fixation: Carbon dioxide is a crucial raw material for the process of photosynthesis. Carbon dioxide is fixed (converted into a form that can be used by the plant) through a series of chemical reactions in the chloroplasts of the plant cells.

3. Water as a solvent: Water (H2O) is a key component in the process of photosynthesis, as it is needed for the reactions that take place in the chloroplasts. Water molecules are also important for the plant's overall function, as they help to transport nutrients and remove waste products.
