--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
Both the Sun and the Moon contribute to the tides on Earth. The Moon's gravitational pull on the Earth is the primary cause of the tides, but the Sun also plays a significant role. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth are aligned (during a new moon or full moon), the Sun's gravitational pull works in conjunction with the Moon's, causing higher than normal high tides and lower than normal low tides. This is known as a spring tide. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth are nearly perpendicular (during a first quarter or third quarter), the Sun's gravitational pull is almost in opposition to the Moon's, resulting in slightly lower high tides and higher low tides, which is known as a neap tide.
