Briefly: what causes ocean tides?
--- TURN 0 ASSISTANT ---
Ocean tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the moon and the sun on the Earth's oceans. The gravitational force of the moon and sun is greater on the side of the Earth facing them, which creates a bulge of water on that side. This creates two high tides and two low tides each day. The strength of the tides is also affected by the shape of the Earth and the distance between the Earth and the Moon.

--- TURN 1 USER ---
And does the Sun contribute too, or only the Moon?
--- TURN 1 ASSISTANT ---
Yes, the Sun also contributes to ocean tides. While the gravitational pull of the Moon is the main cause of ocean tides, the Sun's gravitational force also plays a significant role. The Sun's gravity, although not as strong as the Moon's, still creates a bulge of water on the side of the Earth facing the Sun. This secondary effect can slightly alter the tide patterns, but the Moon's influence remains the dominant factor. The combined effect of the Moon and the Sun's gravitational forces causes the familiar two high tides and two low tides that we experience.

--- TURN 2 USER ---
When do the strongest tides happen during a month?
--- TURN 2 ASSISTANT ---
During a typical month, the strongest tides happen when the Earth, Moon, and Sun are in alignment. This alignment occurs twice per month, once during the first quarter and once during the last quarter of the lunar cycle. During these times, the gravitational forces of the Moon and Sun work together to create the highest tides, which are known as spring tides. This happens at the points in the lunar cycle when the Sun and Moon are on opposite sides of the Earth, causing their gravitational forces to add up.

--- TURN 3 USER ---
What do we call those strong tides?
--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
Those strong tides are called spring tides.
