State the Pythagorean theorem in one line.
--- TURN 0 ASSISTANT ---
The Pythagorean theorem states that in a right-angled triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides.

Certainly! Here's a sketch of a proof of the Pythagorean theorem using rearrangement:

1. Start with a right triangle with sides \(a\), \(b\), and hypotenuse \(c\).
2. Construct squares on each side of the triangle. The area of the square on side \(a\) is \(a^2\), the area of the square on side \(b\) is \(b^2\), and the area of the square on the hypotenuse \(c\) is \(c^2\).
3. Draw lines from the vertices of the triangle to the midpoints of the opposite sides, creating smaller squares and rectangles in the corners. The area of each smaller square is \(\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{a^2}{4} + \frac{b^2}{4}\).
