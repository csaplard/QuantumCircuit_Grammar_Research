Briefly: what causes ocean tides?
--- TURN 0 ASSISTANT ---
Ocean tides are caused by the gravitational forces exerted by the moon and the sun on the Earth's oceans. The gravitational pull of the moon is stronger than that of the sun, so it causes higher tides at the same location throughout the lunar cycle. The sun's gravitational pull is less strong than the moon's, but it still affects the tides, causing them to be higher or lower at different times. The Earth's rotation also contributes to the tides, as the centrifugal force generated by the rotation of the Earth balances the gravitational forces of the moon and the sun.

--- TURN 1 USER ---
And does the Sun contribute too, or only the Moon?
--- TURN 1 ASSISTANT ---
Yes, the Sun also contributes to the tides, but its influence is less significant compared to the Moon. The gravitational pull of the Sun is about 4.2 times stronger than that of the Moon, but the Earth's oceans are not filled with the entire volume of water, so the actual tidal force exerted by the Sun is only about 1/80th of that exerted by the Moon. Additionally, the Moon's orbit around the Earth is tilted at an angle of about 5 degrees, while the Sun's orbit is nearly in line with the Earth's orbit, so the Sun's gravitational influence is more aligned with the Earth's rotational axis, making the Sun's influence more noticeable in some areas of the Earth.

--- TURN 2 USER ---
When do the strongest tides happen during a month?
--- TURN 2 ASSISTANT ---
The strongest tides happen during the new moon and full moon phases of the lunar cycle. At these times, the gravitational forces of the Moon and the Sun are both acting in the same direction, creating the highest tidal peaks. The new moon occurs when the Moon is on the opposite side of the Earth from the Sun, and the full moon occurs when the Moon is on the same side of the Earth as the Sun.

--- TURN 3 USER ---
What do we call those strong tides?
--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
Those strong tides are called spring tides.
