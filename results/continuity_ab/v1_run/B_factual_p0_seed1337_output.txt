Briefly: what causes ocean tides?
--- TURN 0 ASSISTANT ---
Ocean tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the moon and the sun on the Earth's oceans. The gravitational force of the moon and sun is greater on the side of the Earth facing them, which creates a bulge of water on that side. This creates two high tides and two low tides each day. The strength of the tides is also affected by the shape of the Earth and the distance between the Earth and the Moon.

--- TURN 1 USER ---
And does the Sun contribute too, or only the Moon?
--- TURN 1 ASSISTANT ---
The Sun does contribute to the tides, but its effect is less than that of the Moon. While the Moon's gravitational pull is the primary cause of tides, the Sun's gravitational pull also affects the Earth's oceans, contributing to the overall tidal force. The Sun's gravitational pull is about 4.2 times stronger than the Moon's, but its effect is diluted by the fact that the Earth is farther from the Sun than it is from the Moon.

--- TURN 2 USER ---
When do the strongest tides happen during a month?
--- TURN 2 ASSISTANT ---
The strength of tides is affected by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun, as well as the shape of the Earth and the distance between the Earth and the Moon. Generally, the tides are strongest when the Moon and the Sun are in conjunction or opposition, meaning that they are on the same side of the Earth as the Sun. This occurs about every 14 days, and during this time, the Earth, Moon, and Sun are aligned with each other, creating the maximum tidal force.

--- TURN 3 USER ---
What do we call those strong tides?
--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
We call those strong tides "spring tides." Spring tides occur when the Moon, Earth, and Sun are aligned, creating the maximum tidal force and the highest high tides and lowest low tides.
