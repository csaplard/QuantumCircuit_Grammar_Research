--- TURN 0 USER ---
Tell me in two sentences how photosynthesis works.
--- TURN 0 ASSISTANT ---
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. This process occurs primarily in the chloroplasts of plant cells, where chlorophyll captures sunlight, and enzymes catalyze the conversion of carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

--- TURN 1 USER ---
Which wavelengths of light do plants mostly use?
--- TURN 1 ASSISTANT ---
Plants primarily use the wavelengths of light in the blue and red parts of the spectrum for photosynthesis.

--- TURN 2 USER ---
Why do leaves look green if those wavelengths are absorbed?
--- TURN 2 ASSISTANT ---
Leaves appear green because they reflect the green part of the visible light spectrum, which is absorbed by chlorophyll and other pigments in the chloroplasts. The green light is reflected back to our eyes, making the leaves appear green.

--- TURN 3 USER ---
Is there any plant that uses different pigments?
--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
Yes, some plants, particularly the algae and certain bacteria, use different pigments such as chlorophyll a and b, as well as carotenoids and phycobilins, to capture light for photosynthesis. These pigments allow them to use a broader range of wavelengths than just blue and red light.
