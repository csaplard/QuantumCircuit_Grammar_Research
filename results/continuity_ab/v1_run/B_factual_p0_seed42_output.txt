Briefly: what causes ocean tides?
--- TURN 0 ASSISTANT ---
Ocean tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the moon and the sun on the Earth's oceans. The Earth's rotation also plays a role in creating tidal forces. The gravitational force of the moon and sun causes the oceans to bulge out on the side of the Earth facing the moon and the sun, creating high tides. The Earth's rotation causes the bulges to move around the Earth, creating two high tides and two low tides each day.

--- TURN 1 USER ---
And does the Sun contribute too, or only the Moon?
--- TURN 1 ASSISTANT ---
Yes, the Sun also contributes to the formation of ocean tides. The gravitational pull of the Sun on the Earth's oceans is approximately equal to that of the Moon, and it is slightly stronger than the Moon's pull. However, the Moon's gravitational pull is much stronger and causes the more noticeable tidal effects we observe. The Sun's gravitational pull is still important, but it is less significant than the Moon's pull when it comes to generating tides.

--- TURN 2 USER ---
When do the strongest tides happen during a month?
--- TURN 2 ASSISTANT ---
The strongest tides typically occur around the time of new moon and full moon, when the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun is at its strongest. However, the tidal effects can vary depending on the shape of the Earth's rotation and the gravitational pull of other celestial bodies, such as the Sun and the Moon.

--- TURN 3 USER ---
What do we call those strong tides?
--- TURN 3 ASSISTANT ---
Those strong tides are called spring tides. Spring tides occur when the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun are both aligned with the Earth, and they create the highest and lowest tides of the month. The name "spring tides" comes from the fact that they occur around the time of the spring season when the Earth, Moon, and Sun are all in a straight line.
