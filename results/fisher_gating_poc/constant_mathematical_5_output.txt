**1. Set up the Problem**

*   We want to find the area of a circle with radius *r*.
*   We'll use a method called the "method of slicing" or "disk method."
*   We'll imagine the circle as a series of infinitesimally thin slices, each one a disk (or a ring) with a small thickness *dx*.
*   The area of each disk is approximately the area of a rectangle: (width) * (height).  The width is *dx*, and the height is the radius *r*.  So the area of each disk is *r dx*.
*   We'll sum up the areas of all these infinitesimally thin disks from *x = -r* to *x = r* to approximate the total area.

**2. The Integral**

The area *A* of the circle is approximately the sum of the areas of these disks:

A ≈ Σ (Area of each disk) = Σ (r dx)

Since *r* is constant, we can take it out of the integral:

A ≈ r Σ dx

The integral of *dx* is *x*. So,

A ≈ r [x]
