A combustion engine, also known as an internal combustion engine, is a fascinating machine that converts chemical energy into mechanical energy. Here's a breakdown of how it works, step by step, focusing on the most common type – the four-stroke engine:

**1. The Basics: The Goal**

*   The primary goal of a combustion engine is to create controlled explosions that push a piston, which in turn rotates a crankshaft, ultimately powering a vehicle or machine.

**2. The Four Strokes – The Cycle**

The "stroke" refers to the movement of a piston within a cylinder.  A four-stroke engine completes its cycle in four distinct strokes:

**Stroke 1: Intake Stroke**

*   **What Happens:** The piston moves *down* inside the cylinder.
*   **Valve Action:** The *intake valve* opens.
*   **Process:** As the piston moves down, it creates a vacuum, drawing a mixture of air and fuel (in gasoline engines) or just air (in diesel engines) into the cylinder.  Think of it like sucking air into a syringe.

**Stroke 2: Compression Stroke**

*   **What Happens:** The piston moves *up* inside the cylinder.
