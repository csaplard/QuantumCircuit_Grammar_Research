The water cycle, also known as the hydrologic cycle, is a continuous process that describes the movement of water on, above, and below the Earth's surface. It’s a fundamental process that sustains life and plays a crucial role in regulating Earth's climate. Here's a breakdown of the cycle and its significance:

**1. Stages of the Water Cycle:**

* **Evaporation:** This is the process where liquid water changes into water vapor (a gas) and rises into the atmosphere.  It’s primarily driven by the sun's heat, turning water from oceans, lakes, rivers, and even moist soil into vapor.
* **Transpiration:**  This is essentially evaporation from plants. Plants absorb water through their roots and release it into the atmosphere through tiny pores on their leaves.
* **Condensation:** As water vapor rises and cools in the atmosphere, it changes back into liquid water, forming clouds. This happens when water vapor condenses around tiny particles like dust or pollen.
* **Precipitation:** When the water droplets in clouds become too heavy, they fall back to Earth as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.
* **Infiltration:**  Some of the precipitation that reaches the ground
