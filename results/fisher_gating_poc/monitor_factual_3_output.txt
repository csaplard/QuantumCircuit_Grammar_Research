A combustion engine, particularly the internal combustion engine (ICE) found in most cars, works by converting chemical energy (from fuel) into mechanical energy (to move the car). Here's a breakdown of the process, typically in a four-stroke engine:

**1. Intake Stroke:**

*   **Piston Movement:** The piston moves down, creating a vacuum inside the cylinder.
*   **Valve Action:** The intake valve opens, allowing a mixture of air and fuel (in gasoline engines) or just air (in diesel engines) to be drawn into the cylinder.
*   **Result:** The cylinder is now filled with the air-fuel mixture (or just air).

**2. Compression Stroke:**

*   **Piston Movement:** The piston moves *up*, compressing the air-fuel mixture (or just air).
*   **Valve Action:** Both the intake and exhaust valves are closed.
*   **Result:**  Compressing the mixture dramatically increases its temperature and pressure. This is crucial for efficient combustion.

**3. Combustion (Power) Stroke:**

*   **Spark (Gasoline Engines):** At the top of the compression stroke, a spark plug ignites the compressed air-fuel mixture.
