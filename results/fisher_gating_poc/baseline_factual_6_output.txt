Vaccines work by training your immune system to recognize and fight off specific diseases. Here's a breakdown of the process:

**1. Types of Vaccines:**

*   **Live-attenuated vaccines:** These use a weakened (attenuated) form of the disease-causing organism (virus or bacteria).  The weakened organism can still replicate, but it's not strong enough to cause illness in most people.  Examples: MMR (measles, mumps, rubella), chickenpox, rotavirus.
*   **Inactivated vaccines:** These use killed versions of the disease-causing organism.  They can't replicate, so they can't cause illness. Examples: Polio (IPV), Hepatitis A, Influenza (shot).
*   **Subunit, recombinant, polysaccharide, and conjugate vaccines:** These use specific parts of the disease-causing organism, like proteins, sugars, or pieces of the outer coating. They are very safe because they can't cause illness. Examples: Hepatitis B, HPV, Shingles, Pneumococcal.
*   **mRNA vaccines:** These vaccines use messenger RNA (mRNA) to instruct your cells to make a harmless piece of a virus (usually a protein). Your immune system then recognizes this protein
