Vaccines work by teaching your immune system how to recognize and fight off specific diseases without actually causing you to get sick. Here's a breakdown of the process:

**1. Types of Vaccines:**

*   **Live-attenuated vaccines:** These use a weakened (attenuated) form of the live virus or bacteria that causes the disease. Because the pathogen is still alive, it can replicate, but it can't cause serious illness. (Examples: MMR, chickenpox, polio)
*   **Inactivated vaccines:** These use killed versions of the pathogen. Since the pathogen is dead, it can't cause illness. (Examples: Flu shot, polio, hepatitis A)
*   **Subunit, recombinant, polysaccharide, and conjugate vaccines:** These use specific parts of the pathogen, like proteins, sugars, or chemicals. They don't contain any live virus or bacteria. (Examples: Hepatitis B, HPV, shingles)
*   **Toxoid vaccines:** These vaccines use a weakened toxin produced by the bacteria. (Example: Tetanus)
*   **mRNA vaccines:** These vaccines deliver instructions to your cells to make a protein that triggers an immune response. (Example: COVID-19)
