**Causes of World War I**

World War I, also known as the Great War, was a complex conflict with roots extending far beyond a single event. Multiple factors, both long-term and short-term, contributed to its outbreak in 1914. Here's a breakdown of the key causes:

**1. Long-Term Causes (Underlying Tensions):**

* **Militarism:**  Early 20th-century Europe was gripped by an arms race.  Great Britain, France, and Germany were all investing heavily in their military capabilities, believing a strong military was essential for national security and prestige. This led to a climate of suspicion and a willingness to use force.
* **Alliances:** A network of interlocking alliances obligated nations to defend each other in case of attack. These alliances were designed to maintain a balance of power but ultimately dragged Europe into war. The two main alliances were:
    * **The Triple Alliance:** Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy (though Italy later switched sides).
    * **The Triple Entente:** Great Britain, France, and Russia.
* **Imperialism:** European powers were competing for colonies and resources around the globe, particularly in Africa and Asia.
