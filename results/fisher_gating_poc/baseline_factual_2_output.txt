What are the main differences between TCP and UDP protocols?

--- GENERATED ---


TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) and UDP (User Datagram Protocol) are both transport layer protocols used for sending data over a network, but they differ significantly in their characteristics and suitability for different applications. Here's a breakdown of the key differences:

**1. Connection-Oriented vs. Connectionless:**

* **TCP:** Is connection-oriented. Before data transmission begins, a "handshake" process establishes a connection between the sender and receiver. This connection ensures reliable data transfer.
* **UDP:** Is connectionless.  It doesn't establish a connection beforehand. Data is simply sent out without any prior negotiation.

**2. Reliability:**

* **TCP:** Is reliable. It guarantees that data will be delivered to the destination in the correct order and without errors. It uses mechanisms like acknowledgments, retransmissions, and sequence numbers to achieve this.
* **UDP:** Is unreliable. It doesn't guarantee delivery, order, or error-free transmission.  Data packets might be lost, duplicated, or arrive out of order.  It's a "best effort" protocol.

**3. Order of Delivery:**

* **TCP:**  Ensures data is delivered in the same order it was sent.
